Five warning signs when anyone wants your data

Use this checklist on anyone who approaches your company, including us.

  • They ask for raw data before an agreement is signed.
  • They won’t say who ends up with the data or how it’s protected.

Common questions

We’re regulated by MAS. Is this allowed?

It depends on your licence and what would be included. We’ll go through your situation on the call, and nothing goes ahead without sign-off from your compliance team and your own lawyer.

Do we need to send data to find out?

No. Start by telling us what your company does and the kinds of records it keeps. DataSupply doesn’t ask for, receive or store business datasets.

What decides how much it’s worth?

There’s no standard price. Value depends on buyer demand, the quality of your records, what uses are permitted and the rights requested, including any exclusivity. DataSupply helps present and negotiate the opportunity, but the buyer decides whether to proceed and what to pay.

What does it cost us?

Nothing upfront. DataSupply earns a success fee only if a deal it facilitates closes. The fee is set out in the Commercialization Agreement.

Do we give up ownership of our data?

No. A license lets the buyer use agreed records under terms you approve. Your company keeps its data.

Who will we be dealing with?

Me first, Kai Law, Director of Partnerships, DataSupply Singapore. If your company fits, I introduce you to DataSupply’s team. Any agreement is then signed directly between your company and the buyer.

Why would an AI company pay for our records?

AI models have learned from what’s public. How a real company makes decisions, hands work between people and resolves problems is almost never published. Operational records show that in practice, and buyers pay for the ones that are relevant and usable for what they’re building.

How are the AI labs chosen?

DataSupply only works with labs that meet its top 0.01% credibility standard. It looks at each lab, the customers it serves and the kinds of records it needs before suggesting a fit. You’re introduced by name, you verify the lab yourself, and your team and lawyer make the final call.

What kinds of records could matter?

Documented processes, task and project histories, CRM workflow data, templates and frameworks, knowledge bases and everyday working files can all show repeatable patterns. What actually matters depends on the buyer, the quality of your records and how safely they can be prepared.

Do we need to be a technology company?

No. Useful operational knowledge comes from logistics, freight, accounting, law, manufacturing, finance and many other fields. What matters is how your company works and whether its records match what a buyer needs.

Will anyone see our customers’ information?

The programmes involved require sensitive and confidential information to be scrubbed before any use. Ask the buyer to explain its anonymisation and security process in writing, and have your own lawyer review the agreement before anything moves.

How does the deal actually work?

If there’s a fit, you’re introduced to the buyer. You check them out and decide whether to sign an agreement directly with them. Data moves only after you sign, straight from your company to the buyer, and the buyer pays your company directly.

Is there any obligation if we book a call?

None. The call is free, you don’t share any data, and if your company isn’t a fit, I’ll tell you straight.
